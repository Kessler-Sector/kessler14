# Department depth: atmos, cargo, security, service and the rest

Design document. Nothing here is built yet. It sits next to two sibling documents written at the same time:

- `docs/kessler/design/progression.md`: the general progression framework (stages S0–S4, reputation tracks, licences, item rarity tiers, money bands, the `WorkOrders` sector account). This document uses its IDs and numbers; section 8 maps each proposal onto it.
- The medical, chemistry and science design, which covers those departments. They appear here only where other loops feed them.

Scope here: atmospherics and gas mining, cargo and trade, security, salvage and mining, engineering, and the station and service jobs (janitor, chef, bartender, botany, librarian, mail carrier, station staff, station representative).

## 1. Summary

The problem is the same in every department: SS14 gives each job a loop that is complete after an hour or two. Prices are fixed, demand is infinite, and nothing remembers what happened yesterday. On a persistent server the loops either run out of interest (cargo, service) or of resistance (gas mining).

Top recommendations, in build order:

1. **Fix the numbers first.** Gas mining pays far more than anything else: a 52,000-credit Gaslight can drain one very large plasma deposit worth about 65,000 credits at Dusk in under fifteen minutes. A trade crate earns 600 profit, and a meal sells for about 30. Mail carriers and station staff earn nothing directly. Rebalance before the server opens, while nobody is attached to the current rates.
2. **Build three shared systems once and use them everywhere** (section 5):
   - **Sector market ledger**: sale prices fall as a station is flooded with one commodity and recover over real time. This is the fix for gas and it is also what makes trade routes interesting.
   - **Contract board**: NPC-issued work orders and contracts with requirements, a real-time deadline, a payout to the player, and reputation. It gives every department work when nobody else is online, and it is the backbone for station jobs at lowpop.
   - **Station needs and the sector threat level**: stations consume things over real time (food, air, medicine, power, cleanliness) and post contracts to restock them. NPC pirates and smugglers grow in strength when nobody deals with them, which gives security PvE work and gives haulers and miners a reason to want security.
3. **Pay the worker, not the account.** Mail, power sales and dead-drop scans pay into station bank accounts today, and those accounts also tick up on a timer whether anyone works or not. Contracts should pay the player who did the work from the framework's population-scaled `WorkOrders` account, with a smaller cut to the department account.
4. **Gas mining: from "find rock, drain rock" to an atmos trade.** Raw gas cheap and saturating, refined gas (tritium, frezon, pure canisters to spec) valuable, rich deposits in hazardous fields, deposits that run out for good while new fields appear over time.
5. **Cargo: routes that change.** Each station has supply and demand that drifts over days. Supply chains link departments (botany → kitchen → remote outposts, gas → stations, ore → Theseus). Courier contracts carry collateral, and valuable cargo draws NPC pirates.
6. **Security: PvE first, PvP when it comes.** An NPC crime director scaled to how many security players are online: smuggler ships to intercept, pirate raids on haulers, patrol and escort contracts, generated investigations that use the existing forensics, and licence and customs checks. The current combat events need 15 players, so lowpop security has no NPC content at all today.
7. **Service jobs: a Dusk work-order board.** Messes, diners, drink orders, produce orders and parcels generated by NPC traffic and paid per job. A new player can earn a starter income alone on an empty server and build Dusk reputation towards their first ship.

The open decisions for the owner are in section 10.

## 2. Design principles for this server

These follow from KESSLER.md (persistence, about 50 players and often fewer, restarts every few days, offline players stay in the world).

1. **Work must exist without other players.** Any loop that needs a criminal, a customer or a crew to be online fails at lowpop. NPC demand is the base; other players add to it.
2. **Every faucet needs a sink, and every source needs a limit.** Fixed prices with infinite demand mean the best money-per-hour activity wins forever. Diminishing returns (saturation, depletion, contract caps) keep several activities worth doing.
3. **Real time, not round time.** Persistent state uses `DateTime` UTC, like upkeep does (`ShipOwnershipComponent.NextUpkeep`). `IGameTiming.CurTime` resets at every restart, and `Timer.Spawn` callbacks are lost. A deadline or a decay that uses either breaks across a restart.
4. **Departments should need each other.** A loop is more durable when its inputs come from another player's work and its outputs feed a third. Section 7 maps these links.
5. **Never punish the offline.** NPC threats may hit ships with crew aboard, never stored ships or SSD players' property. Contracts expire without penalty when the holder is offline, or pause.
6. **Reuse what exists.** The pirate bounty database, the upstream cargo bounty system, community goals, forensics, station records, the autopilot and dead drops are all working pieces. New systems should be thin layers over them.

### 3.2 Atmospherics and gas mining

Files: `Content.Server/_NF/Atmos/Systems/GasDepositSystem.cs`, `Content.Shared/_NF/Atmos/Components/GasDepositComponent.cs`, `Content.Shared/_NF/Atmos/Components/GasDepositExtractorComponent.cs`, `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Atmos/deposits.yml`, `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Entities/Objects/Specific/Atmospherics/deposits.yml`, `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Entities/Structures/Piping/Atmospherics/unary.yml`, `Resources/Prototypes/Atmospherics/gases.yml`, `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Atmospherics/gases.yml`, `Content.Server/Atmos/EntitySystems/AtmosphereSystem.Utils.cs` (`GetPrice`).

How it works:

- Gas deposits spawn on asteroid debris (`Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Entities/World/Debris/asteroids.yml`): rock asteroids get up to 2 deposits from oxygen, nitrogen, CO2, plasma, air and mixed; ice asteroids up to 4 (oxygen, nitrogen, air, water vapour, mostly large and very large); andesite up to 3 nitrous oxide.
- A deposit holds a fixed gas mixture rolled at map init. A normal "mostly plasma" deposit has 15,000–20,000 mol plasma plus up to about 9,000 mol of other gases. Size multipliers: very small 0.3, small 0.6, normal 1.0, large 1.4, very large 2.7.
- The gas mining drill (`GasMiningDrill`) extracts 75 mol/s (Wayfarer raised it from 50), times 1.2 per machine part tier above 1 (about 130 mol/s with tier 4 parts), up to the pipe's target pressure.
- Gas is sold by piping it into a `GasSalePoint` next to a `GasSaleConsole`. The sale point has unlimited internal storage. The console sells everything at `pricePerMole`, **ignoring purity**, times a market modifier: 1.25 (`GasSaleConsoleHigh`) at Dusk, the cargo depots and Edison, 1.0 at Theseus.
- Prices per mole: frezon 8, tritium 4, helium 2.5, plasma 1, CO2 / water vapour / N2O 0.5, oxygen / nitrogen / respiron 0.2, ammonia 0.15.
- Ships with drills: Gaslight (52,000, medium), Retort (106,000), Gasbender (138,000), Ambition (278,000), Manta (281,000).

Worked example: a very large plasma deposit is about 52,000 credits of gas, 65,000 at a 1.25 console. It holds about 60,000 mol, which a base drill empties in about 13 minutes. The cheapest gas ship costs 52,000 and pays 520 a day in upkeep. **One good deposit pays for the ship.**

Why it gets boring and why it is too profitable:

- Price is fixed and demand is infinite. Selling 1,000,000 mol of plasma pays exactly as well as the first 1,000.
- Skill is not rewarded. Purity is ignored at the sale console, so there is no reason to filter, mix or react gases. The raw rock is the product.
- No hazard. Drilling is as safe as a pump.
- Depletion is real (the remaining mixture is a `[DataField]` and survives saves), but the sector keeps generating debris further out, so the answer to depletion is "fly further", not "do something different".

Persistence gaps:

- `GasSalePointComponent.GasStorage` is `[ViewVariables]`, not a data field. Gas piped into a sale point but not sold yet is lost at every restart.
- `GasDepositComponent.LowMoles` is not saved, so after a load the "low" indicator never shows (it is only set in map init). Minor.

### 3.3 Cargo, trade and mail

Files: `Content.Server/_NF/Cargo/Systems/NFCargoSystem*.cs` (orders, pallets, trade crates, pirate bounties), `Content.Server/_WF/Cargo/Systems/WFCargoSystem.cs`, `Content.Server/_NF/Market/` (market console, crate machine), `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Entities/Structures/Specific/TradingCrates/`, `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Catalog/Cargo/cargo_cargo.yml`, `Resources/Prototypes/_WF/Catalog/merchant_uplink_catalogue.yml`, `Content.Server/_DV/Mail/`, `Content.Server/Cargo/Systems/CargoSystem.Bounty.cs` (upstream, unused here).

How it works:

- **Trade crates** are bought from cargo for 1,000 and labelled with a random destination station (cargo depots, the trade mall, Theseus). At the destination they sell for 1,600 (400 elsewhere) and give 2 merchant tokens. Express crates sell for 2,200, plus 500 if delivered within 28 minutes. Contraband trade crates (5,000–8,000) exist for the black market.
- **Merchant tokens** buy fixed items at the merchant trading uplink (hyposprays, vending restocks, techfab boards, an RTG flatpack, and so on).
- **Pallets** sell anything with a price at cargo; the console's `MarketModifier` scales it. Sold items go into the grid's `CargoMarketDataComponent`, and other players can buy them back at the **market console**. This is a real second-hand market, but with fixed prices.
- **Mail** (`MailSystem.cs`): parcels teleport in for players online at the station; opening one deposits 7,500 into the **Frontier sector account** (`LedgerEntryType.MailDelivered`). The carrier gets nothing. There is no penalty.
- **Hauler autopilot event** (`Content.Server/_WF/StationEvents/Events/HaulerAutopilotRule.cs`): an armoured transport flies itself across the sector, 10+ players only, at most every 5 hours.
- The upstream cargo bounty system exists in the code but no bounty console is mapped at Dusk.

Why it gets boring: the trade crate loop is the same every time. Destinations are random but values are flat, there is no information to gather, no risk on most routes, and no demand that changes. Express crates are the only time pressure, and it is a timer, not a decision.

Persistence gaps:

- `TradeCrateComponent.ExpressDeliveryTime` is not a data field and the express flag is cleared by a `Timer.Spawn`. After a restart an express crate keeps its priority look but has no deadline: no bonus and no penalty.
- Mail in flight survives as entities, but the teleporter's schedule is runtime state.

### 3.4 Security

Files: `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/PointsOfInterest/nfsd.yml` (the CGP outpost: Sheriff, Bailiff, Senior Officer, Brigmedic, Detective, 3 Deputies), `Content.Server/_WF/Outlaws/Systems/WantedOutlawSystem.cs`, `Content.Server/_WF/OutlawObjectives/`, `Content.Server/_NF/Smuggling/DeadDropSystem.cs`, `Content.Server/_NF/Contraband/Systems/ContrabandTurnInSystem.cs`, `Content.Server/_NF/BountyContracts/`, `Content.Server/Forensics/Systems/ForensicScannerSystem.cs`, `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/GameRules/roundstart.yml`, `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Events/nf_bluespace_grids_events.yml`.

What gives security work today:

- **Player criminals.** Outlaw jobs spawn already Wanted and are announced on the CGP radio. Perdition has 8 pirate captain and 8 pirate slots. Outlaw objectives pay outlaws for crimes against players: kill 100,000, theft 150,000, ship damage 200,000, permanent removal 250,000, ship theft 400,000 (plus DC).
- **Dead drops** (`nf14.smuggling.*`): contraband pods appear at random points 6.5–8 km out every 15–90 minutes. Scanning a dead drop pays the CGP account and FUC. This is the only NPC-generated crime.
- **Contraband turn-in**: confiscated contraband sold at the contraband pallet for FUC, which buys security gear.
- **Bounty contracts**: a PDA board where players post bounties on other players.
- **Combat events**: Syndicate FTL interception, Wizard Federation scout and Blood Moon all need **15 players minimum** and come at most every 1–1.5 hours, the first after 2 hours. On a lowpop server none of them run.

So the owner's reading is right: without a criminal player, security has dead drops and nothing else.

Persistence gaps:

- Outlaw objective state (`OutlawObjectiveComponent`, `OutlawShipDamageComponent`, `ForgedDeedComponent`) is all `[ViewVariables]`: active objectives, pending items and credit for ship damage are lost at restart.
- Dead drop timers (`DeadDropComponent.NextDrop`) use `CurTime`.
- Pirate bounties are saved (`SectorPirateBountyDatabaseComponent`, data fields with `TimeOffsetSerializer`).
- Bounty contracts are a `FrozenDictionary` data field; whether it round-trips needs a test.

### 3.6 Engineering and power

Files: `Content.Server/_NF/Power/` (`PowerTransmissionComponent`), `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Entities/Structures/Power/transmission_point.yml`, `Content.Server/_WF/Atmos/` (inline heat exchanger).

- Power transmission points buy power on a log curve up to 150 credits/s, but pay the **Edison sector account**, not the engineer. Edison is not a required POI on Wayfarer.
- There is no maintenance demand: stations do not break unless a player or a meteor swarm breaks them.

### 3.7 Station and service jobs

Dusk (`Resources/Prototypes/_WF/Maps/Outpost/dusk.yml`) offers: Station Representative (1), Janitor (5), Mail Carrier (5), Station Staff (10, "keep Dusk and Calypso clean, and their guests entertained and fed"), Director of Care (1), ER medic (unlimited), and contractors and borgs. Theseus has a Plant Manager and 2–4 Plant Technicians. Club Calypso has the bar.

None of these jobs has an income of its own. A station representative can withdraw from the Frontier sector account at the station ATM (`Content.Server/_NF/Bank/StationATMSystem.cs`) and hand out pay, so staff are paid only when a representative is online and willing. Food and drink sell for almost nothing at cargo. The library console (`Content.Server/_NF/Library/`) stores player-written books in the database. Pest migrations (`Resources/Prototypes/_NF/GameRules/pests.yml`) bring mice and cockroaches.

This is the biggest problem for the owner's plan that new players spend their first days on station jobs: at lowpop a new player at Dusk has nobody to serve and no pay for doing it.

### 3.8 Sector bank accounts

`Content.Server/_NF/Bank/BankSystem.Sector.cs` ticks each account up every second of game time (Frontier 18,000/h, CGP 72,000/h, Medical 21,600/h, Edison 18,000/h) from a 50,000 start. Balances are saved and have no cap: every 14 hours the server runs with someone online (it pauses when empty) adds about a million to the CGP account alone. That money is only useful if a head is online to spend it, and it does not depend on whether anyone works. Section 5.2 proposes paying contracts from these accounts so the faucet is tied to work.

## 4. Lessons from other games

| Game | What it does | Lesson for us |
|---|---|---|
| **EVE Online** (hauling, industry, mining) | Regional markets with player buy and sell orders; courier contracts with collateral; ore and gas sites that deplete and respawn elsewhere; refining skill; industry cost scales with how busy a system is | Profit comes from information (where prices are) and risk (collateral, ganking), not from the act of flying. Depletion plus respawn elsewhere keeps miners moving. Activity-based cost scaling is a natural saturation mechanic. |
| **Star Wars Galaxies** (resources) | Resources spawn with random quality stats for one to three weeks, then vanish; surveying finds concentration | Rotating "this week's good deposit" gives miners a reason to log in and a scouting skill. Quality matters, not only volume. Direct model for gas fields. |
| **Elite Dangerous** | Supply and demand per station; prices fall as you sell; mission boards per faction with reputation; resource extraction sites with NPC pirates and police | Per-station saturation makes traders spread out. Faction mission boards give lone players structured work. NPC pirates at mining sites tie mining to combat. |
| **Starsector** | Colonies with shortages that raise prices; bounties on named NPC fleets; faction relations | Shortages are events that pull players to a place. Named NPC targets are more interesting than random spawns. |
| **Barotrauma** | Outposts with per-faction reputation, store prices that improve with reputation, mission types (cargo, escort, pirates, salvage, beacons), abandoned outposts with bandits | A small set of contract types with reputation behind them carries a whole campaign for 1–8 players. Reputation as a discount is a soft, non-grindy reward. |
| **Space Engineers** (economy and factions) | NPC faction stations with contracts (hauling, escort, search, hunt, repair), stock that changes, reputation per faction | Contracts generated by NPC stations are enough to keep a mostly-empty server alive. |
| **Sea of Thieves** (trading companies) | Separate companies for cargo, treasure and combat, each with its own reputation and rewards | One reputation track per kind of work lets players specialise and makes each department feel like a career. |
| **SS13 Goonstation** (cargo) | Shipping market whose prices shift over time; special requisitions with bonuses | Even in a round-based game, moving prices make cargo players watch the market. |
| **SS13 lowpop security** (various servers) | Security idles without antagonists; servers that work add NPC threats, patrol duties, or merge security with other jobs at low population | Security needs regular non-player work: patrols, inspections, NPC incidents. At very low population security is better as a contract any armed player can take than as a staffed department. |
| **Shiptest (SS13)** | A Frontier-like ship game with outposts, mission boards and factions | Close analogue; mission boards and factions are what kept it going between player interactions. |

The common pattern: **generated demand + diminishing returns + reputation + risk**. None of the successful designs rely on other players being present for the base loop.

## 5. Shared systems

These are built once and every department uses them. Each one lives in `Content.Server/_Kessler/Economy/` (new) and keeps its state on the sector service entity (`Content.Server/_NF/SectorServices/`) or on station entities, so it is saved with the sector.

### 5.1 Sector market ledger (saturation and demand)

What it does: every sale point (cargo pallet console, gas sale console, contraband pallet, market console purchases) belongs to a station. For each station and commodity the ledger keeps a **saturation** value. Selling adds to it; it decays over real time. The sale price is the base price times a demand factor times a saturation factor.

Suggested formula, simple to reason about:

- Saturation `S` decays exponentially with a half-life `H` (real time).
- Price multiplier for the next unit: `1 / (1 + S / K)`, with a floor (for example 0.2).
- Revenue for selling `Q` units into an unsaturated market is `base × K × ln(1 + Q / K)`. Selling `K` units earns 69% of the flat price; selling `4K` earns 40% on average.

Example for gas with `K` = 50,000 mol per gas per station and `H` = 24 hours: one very large plasma deposit sold at Dusk earns about 72% of today's price. A second one the same day earns about 45%. Taking the second load to a cargo depot earns full price, which gives gas miners a reason to haul and a reason to talk to cargo.

Commodities are coarse categories, not prototypes: each gas, ore and refined metals, food, drink, medicine, trade goods, salvage. Category comes from tags or a small component on the prototype (default "general goods", with no saturation, so nothing breaks).

Demand factor: each station has a demand value per category that drifts slowly (random walk over days) and jumps on events (a shortage at Theseus). Stations publish their current prices on a **market board** (a PDA cartridge or console). Seeing prices at a station you are not at could be gated by a licence or by docking there recently, so price information is worth something.

- Effort: **medium**.
- Code: new `MarketLedgerSystem`; hooks in `NFCargoSystem.Pallet.cs` (`SellPallets`), `GasDepositSystem.OnConsoleSell`, `ContrabandTurnInSystem`, and `MarketSystem` for buying. Each is a one-line call with a `// Kessler` marker, or a raised event.
- Depends on: nothing. Rates per stage come from the progression framework.
- Persistence: `Dictionary<(station, category), LedgerEntry>` data field on each station entity or the sector service entity, with `LastUpdate` as `DateTime` UTC. Decay is applied lazily when read, so restarts and pauses need no special handling.

### 5.2 Contract board

What it does: a sector-wide list of contracts issued by NPC factions (Dusk administration, CGP, Theseus, the cargo depots, the medical service, the merchant guild, and the pirates for outlaws). A contract has:

- an issuer (faction and station), a type, requirements, a reward (credits, reputation, sometimes items or tokens), a real-time deadline, a minimum stage or licence, and an optional collateral;
- a holder (character profile id) once accepted, or "open to anyone" for small jobs;
- a completion check that is a server event, not a UI button.

Contract types (each department section below lists its own):

| Type | Completion check | Existing piece to reuse |
|---|---|---|
| Procurement: deliver N of X | Items sold on the issuer's pallet or delivered to a pad | Pirate bounty database (`NFCargoSystem.PirateBounty.cs`), upstream cargo bounties |
| Delivery / courier | A tagged crate sold at the destination | Trade crates (`NFCargoSystem.TradeCrates.cs`) |
| Gas to spec | Canister contents checked at a gas sale point | `GasDepositSystem` sale point, `GasMixture` |
| Kill or disable NPC target | Death or grid destruction of a tagged entity | Community goal kill orders (`CommunityGoalsSystem.RecordKill`), expedition elimination |
| Patrol / visit | Holder's ship within range of waypoints in order | Pinpointer, radar |
| Escort | Escorted grid arrives intact with escort nearby | Autopilot (`Content.Server/_WF/Shuttles/Systems/AutopilotSystem.cs`) |
| Investigation | Correct suspect named and arrested, evidence delivered | Forensics, station and criminal records |
| Station work order | A tile cleaned, a dish delivered, a machine repaired | Puddles, food, construction |

Payment goes **to the holder's bank** via `BankSystem`, from the framework's `WorkOrders` sector account (its tick rate scales with online players, which bounds the faucet), with a cut (for example 10–20%) to the issuing department's sector account. The departments' own flat ticking income is reduced or capped, so their balances reflect work done. The ledger type `StationWithdrawalWorkOrder` already exists in the bank code and is unused; contract payouts can use it or a new `ContractPayout` type.

Sizing follows the framework: payout = expected minutes × the holder's stage band rate (S0 about 150/min, S2 about 500/min), and reputation = about 1 point per expected minute on the issuer's track, awarded to every participant (the whole crew for ship jobs).

Caps keep it from becoming the new gas mining: each issuer has a budget per real day per category, and per-character limits on how many of one type can be done per day, tuned by stage. The framework's daily reputation soft cap (180 per track per real day) applies on top.

Other designs plug in as contract types rather than separate boards: the medical-science design's pharmacy contract board is the "procurement" type with a reagent check and the `Medical` track.

UI: a PDA cartridge (`Content.Client/_Kessler/Contracts/`) plus a console at each station. The bounty contract cartridge (`Content.Server/_NF/BountyContracts/`) already has a contract list UI to copy from.

- Effort: **large** (the board itself is medium; each contract type is small to medium on top).
- Depends on: the framework's `KesslerReputationSystem.TryAward`, licence checks and `WorkOrders` account. Can ship with a stub (no gating, no reputation) first.
- Persistence: contracts are `[DataDefinition]` records in a list on the sector service entity, with `DateTime` deadlines. Tagged entities (a delivery crate, an NPC target) carry a small component with the contract id. Deadlines that pass while the server is down resolve at load. A held contract whose holder is offline pauses or quietly expires without penalty (principle 5).

### 5.3 Station needs

What it does: each station has stocks of a few needs that drain over real time and are restocked by players: food and drink, medicine, air (canisters), power or fuel, cleanliness, materials. When a stock is low the station posts procurement contracts and its demand factor in the ledger rises. When it is full, contracts stop. A neglected station shows it: vending machines run low, lights fail, a mess builds up, prices rise.

This turns "infinite demand at a fixed price" into "real, finite demand that comes back every day", and it is what makes service jobs and supply chains worth doing at lowpop.

- Effort: **medium**.
- Code: `StationNeedsComponent` on station entities (`Content.Server/_Kessler/Economy/`); posts contracts through 5.2; ties into vending machines (`Content.Server/VendingMachines/VendingMachineSystem.cs`, already taxed into sector accounts) for restock.
- Depends on: 5.1 and 5.2.
- Persistence: need levels plus `DateTime` of last update on the station entity; drain applied lazily.

### 5.4 Sector threat level and NPC factions

What it does: tracks the strength of a few NPC factions (pirate clans, smuggling rings, and later others) as numbers that grow over real time and shrink when players beat them. The threat level decides how often and how hard NPC incidents happen, and where. A director spawns incidents through the existing game-rule scheduler (`BasicStationEventScheduler`), scaled by **how many players are online and how many of them are armed or security**, not by a fixed minimum.

- Incidents: smuggler ship runs, pirate raids on NPC freighters, pirate ambushes near gas fields and trade lanes, distress calls, a pirate outpost appearing as a POI, a named pirate captain with a bounty.
- Feedback: ignored pirates grow (more raids, bigger ships, raids closer to stations); beaten pirates retreat. Gas fields and trade routes far from the CGP outpost have higher threat, so risk tracks reward.
- Owned ships: raids only target ships with crew aboard and never stored ships, docked ships or SSD bodies (principle 5).

- Effort: **large** (director medium; NPC ships that fight is the hard part, see 6.3).
- Code: `Content.Server/_Kessler/Threat/`; reuses `AutopilotSystem`, `HaulerAutopilotRule`, bluespace event prototypes, NPC HTN (`Content.Server/_WF/NPC/HTN`), pirate bounty and outlaw objective code for rewards.
- Depends on: 5.2 for bounties and escort contracts; framework for reputation with CGP and the pirates.
- Persistence: faction strength and `DateTime` on the sector service entity. Spawned NPC grids are ordinary entities in the save; a tagging component ties them to their faction so the director can count them after a load.

## 6. Department proposals

Each proposal has an effort (small: days; medium: one to two weeks; large: more), the code it touches, its dependencies and how its state survives a restart.

### 6.1 Atmospherics and gas mining

Goal: gas mining stays the high-reward, skill-heavy job, but the money comes from atmos knowledge and logistics, not from finding a rock.

**A1. Rebalance raw gas now.** Cut `pricePerMole` for raw gases that deposits produce in bulk (plasma 1 → about 0.4; oxygen and nitrogen 0.2 → 0.1; water vapour, CO2, N2O lower), keep refined gases (tritium, frezon) high, and lower `GasSaleConsoleHigh` from 1.25 to 1.0 everywhere except where a station needs gas. Target, against the framework's money bands: the Gaslight is a Medium ship (stage S2, 25–40k per hour). With plasma at 0.4 a very large plasma deposit is worth about 20–22k; counting search, travel and drilling at roughly one deposit an hour, raw mining lands at the bottom of the S2 band. Refining and gas contracts (A4, A5) reach the top of S2 and into S3. Today's 65k per deposit is above the S4 band.
- Effort: **small** (YAML with `# Kessler` markers).
- Code: `Resources/Prototypes/Atmospherics/gases.yml`, `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Atmospherics/gases.yml`, `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Entities/Structures/Machines/Computers/computers.yml`.
- Depends on: the framework's money bands (S2: 25–40k/h).
- Persistence: none.

**A2. Saturation on gas sales.** Gas sale consoles use the market ledger (5.1), one category per gas, per station. Selling a whole deposit at one station drops its price there; other stations still pay full. This alone ends the "drain and sell at Dusk forever" loop and creates hauling work.
- Effort: **small** once 5.1 exists.
- Code: `GasDepositSystem.OnConsoleSell` and `UpdateConsoleInterface` (show the price per gas and the current saturation).
- Depends on: 5.1.
- Persistence: in the ledger.

**A3. Purity and specification pay.** Stop ignoring purity at the sale console, or pay a premium for gas at a stated purity. The gas sale UI shows "unrefined mixture: 40% of value", so filtering and separating gases becomes the job. Canister contracts (A4) pay per specification.
- Effort: **small**.
- Code: `GasDepositSystem` (`GetPrice(..., ignorePurity: true)` → a purity curve).
- Depends on: nothing.
- Persistence: none.

**A4. Gas contracts.** NPC orders through the contract board: "10 canisters of oxygen at 4,500 kPa for Dusk life support", "2,000 mol of frezon below -150 °C for the medical service", "tritium for the CGP outpost's reactor", "pure nitrogen for Theseus". Delivered by canister (so haulers can carry them) or piped at a sale point. Station needs (5.3) post most of these.
- Effort: **medium**.
- Code: a "gas to spec" contract type checking `GasMixture` (moles, purity, temperature, pressure) on canisters sold at a pallet or a gas sale point.
- Depends on: 5.2, 5.3.
- Persistence: contract records; canisters are ordinary saved entities (`GasCanisterComponent.Air` is saved).

**A5. Refined gas is where the money is.** Tritium (plasma burned lean with oxygen), frezon (tritium, nitrogen, cold) and later new products are made in reaction chambers, which atmos players already know from upstream SS14. With raw gas cheap (A1) and refined gas valuable and contract-demanded (A4), a gas ship becomes a refinery: drill, separate, react, cool, deliver. This is the depth the owner asked for, and it uses mechanics that exist. Optionally add one or two Kessler gases or products (for example a fuel-grade mixture for a future ship fuel mechanic) whose recipe requires several stages.
- Effort: **small** for pricing; **medium** per new gas or reaction.
- Code: prices only; new reactions in `Resources/Prototypes/_Kessler/Atmospherics/` (`GasReactionPrototype`).
- Depends on: A1, A4. Coordinate with the med-sci design if frezon or new gases are medical inputs.
- Persistence: none.

**A6. Gas fields that rotate, and hazards.** Instead of deposits only on random debris, the sector spawns **gas fields**: clusters of deposits around a nebula or a gas giant marker, each lasting one to three real weeks, then fading (SWG model). A field's composition and richness are rolled and announced loosely ("rich helium readings near ..."). Rich fields carry hazards:
- *volatile deposits* that ignite if extracted too hot or too fast (the drill's output temperature and rate become decisions);
- *pressurised pockets* that blow out when drilled without venting, damaging the ship;
- *radiation* or *hot* fields that need shielding and cooling;
- higher NPC pirate threat (5.4).

Gas deposit scanners (`GasDepositScannerSystem`) become a scouting skill: find the field's best pockets before others.
- Effort: **large**.
- Code: new field POI generation in `Content.Server/_Kessler/Atmos/` using the worldgen debris system (`Resources/Prototypes/_NF/Entities/World/Debris/`); hazard components on deposits; `GasDepositSystem.OnExtractorUpdate` raises an event each tick that hazard components handle (keeps the upstream edit to one line).
- Depends on: 5.4 for pirates; framework rarity tiers for which gases count as rare.
- Persistence: the field is an entity with a `DateTime` expiry; deposits are already saved. Expired fields are deleted at load or at their expiry. Never delete a field while a ship is docked to it; mark it "depleted" instead.

**A7. Extraction licence (framework).** Commercial gas sales above a daily volume need a licence (`GasExtractionI`: `Freight` L1; `GasExtractionII`: `Freight` L2 and `Engineering` L1, section 8); higher tiers open rich fields and better contracts. Unlicensed sales still work but at a black-market discount and with a chance to flag the ship for CGP inspection (6.3, S4), which gives security a regulatory job.
- Effort: **small** once the framework has licences.
- Code: check in `OnConsoleSell`.
- Depends on: framework licences.
- Persistence: in the framework's character data.

### 6.2 Cargo, trade and mail

Goal: cargo is about knowing where things are needed, taking risks to get them there, and linking other departments, not about buying the same crate.

**C1. Station markets that drift.** With the ledger (5.1), every station has a demand value per category that drifts over real days, plus shortages from events and station needs. The market board shows each station's buy prices. Haulers buy goods where they are cheap (player-sold goods at the market console, or NPC wholesale crates) and sell where they are needed. Trade crates become **commodity crates** of a category (food, medical, industrial, luxury, gas canisters) whose value depends on where they are sold, rather than one fixed destination.
- Effort: **medium**.
- Code: `NFCargoSystem.TradeCrates.cs` (price from the ledger, not `ValueAtDestination`), new crate prototypes in `Resources/Prototypes/_Kessler/Cargo/`, a market board cartridge.
- Depends on: 5.1.
- Persistence: ledger; crates are entities. Fix the express deadline (C6).

**C2. Supply-chain contracts.** Procurement and delivery contracts that connect departments, posted by station needs (5.3) and NPC factions:
- botany produce → Dusk kitchen; meals and drinks → remote POIs, the CGP outpost, mining outposts;
- ore and refined metals → Theseus fabrication; plates and parts → stations that need repair;
- gas canisters → stations' life support;
- medicine (from the med-sci loops) → stations and the CGP outpost;
- salvage and data disks → science.

Cargo is the department that moves everything, so every other department's output becomes a cargo job.
- Effort: **medium** (on top of 5.2 and 5.3, mostly prototypes).
- Code: contract prototypes in `Resources/Prototypes/_Kessler/Contracts/`.
- Depends on: 5.2, 5.3; the other departments' products.
- Persistence: contracts.

**C3. Courier contracts with collateral.** High-value deliveries require the hauler to put up collateral (EVE model), refunded on delivery. Collateral turns theft into a real risk, makes reputation valuable (higher reputation lowers the collateral), and gives pirates, NPC or player, something to hunt. Players can also post courier contracts for each other (a player shop owner restocking from Theseus), which the planned POI shops will need.
- Effort: **medium**.
- Code: contract type with a bank hold through `BankSystem`; sealed crate prototype that reports tampering.
- Depends on: 5.2, framework reputation.
- Persistence: collateral recorded in the contract (and debited in the database immediately, matching KESSLER.md's rule that bank changes are written at once).

**C4. Convoys and risk.** Valuable contracts spawn or route through higher-threat space; NPC pirate interdictions (5.4) target loaded freighters with crew aboard. NPC freighters (the existing armoured transport event, made lowpop-friendly) run real routes and post escort contracts. This is what links cargo to security.
- Effort: **medium** for routing NPC freighters with `AutopilotSystem`; NPC attackers are covered in 6.3.
- Code: `Content.Server/_WF/StationEvents/Events/HaulerAutopilotRule.cs` as a base; new rule in `Content.Server/_Kessler/Threat/`.
- Depends on: 5.4.
- Persistence: freighters are grids in the save with a component holding destination and contract id. `AutopilotComponent.TargetCoordinates` is a data field, but it is a `MapCoordinates`, whose `MapId` is a runtime id; store the destination as an entity (the target station) instead, and test it across a load.

**C5. Mail that pays the carrier.** Opening a parcel pays the carrier (or splits between carrier and the Frontier account) instead of 7,500 to the account only. Add NPC parcels for POIs and remote stations so a mail carrier at an empty Dusk has a route: a small courier shuttle run to the cargo depots and Theseus. Mail for offline players waits at a post office locker at Dusk (it is already a saved entity) instead of being dropped.
- Effort: **small** for the payout; **medium** for NPC routes.
- Code: `Content.Server/_DV/Mail/EntitySystems/MailSystem.cs` (payout line, with a `// Kessler` marker), mail pools in prototypes.
- Depends on: 5.2 if NPC parcels are contracts.
- Persistence: parcels are entities; recipient by name today should become profile id for offline players.

**C6. Persistence fixes.** Express crate deadline as `DateTime` UTC data field; no `Timer.Spawn`.
- Effort: **small**.
- Code: `Content.Shared/_NF/Trade/TradeCrateComponent.cs`, `NFCargoSystem.TradeCrates.cs`.
- Persistence: that is the fix.

### 6.3 Security

Goal: security has a day's work with zero criminal players, and gets more interesting when criminals are around. Security work protects the economy, so other players want security to exist.

**S1. Lowpop-scaled NPC incidents.** Replace the fixed 15-player minimum on combat events with scaling by online security and armed players (5.4). Small versions run at 3–5 players: a single pirate skiff, a smuggler shuttle, a distress call. Bigger versions at higher population.
- Effort: **small** to change thresholds (YAML, `nf_bluespace_grids_events.yml`); **medium** for scaling logic in the director.
- Code: event prototypes; `Content.Server/_Kessler/Threat/`.
- Depends on: 5.4 for scaling.
- Persistence: event grids are saved entities; rule state that matters (reward pending) moves to data fields.

**S2. Smuggler interception.** Dead drops already generate NPC smuggling. Extend it: NPC smuggler shuttles fly (autopilot) from a dead drop to a black-market point. CGP can track them from dead-drop hints, intercept, board, and seize cargo. Seized contraband pays the officer (contract) and FUC. Escaped smugglers raise the smuggling ring's strength.
- Effort: **medium**.
- Code: `Content.Server/_NF/Smuggling/DeadDropSystem.cs` (raise an event when a pod spawns; the new system reacts), `AutopilotSystem`.
- Depends on: 5.4.
- Persistence: dead-drop `NextDrop` to `DateTime`; smuggler grids are entities.

**S3. Pirate raids and named bounties.** Pirate clans (5.4) send raiders against NPC freighters and against player ships working in high-threat areas (gas fields, far trade lanes). Each clan has named captains with bounties that grow over time; killing or capturing one lowers that clan's strength for days. Pirate outposts appear as POIs when a clan is strong, and become a raid target for a group.

The hard part is NPC ships that fight. Options, cheapest first:
1. Raiders as boarding parties: an NPC skiff docks or breaches and NPC crew (existing hostile humanoid mobs with HTN) board. No ship-to-ship AI needed.
2. Turreted NPC ships that hold position or follow the autopilot and fire automatic turrets at hostile IFF in range.
3. Proper ship combat AI. Large; port from another fork if one exists with a compatible licence (check Mono and others).

Start with 1 and 2.
- Effort: **large** overall.
- Code: `Content.Server/_Kessler/Threat/`, NPC prototypes in `Resources/Prototypes/_Kessler/`, `AutopilotSystem`, `Content.Server/_WF/NPC/HTN`.
- Depends on: 5.4, 5.2.
- Persistence: clan strength and captains as data on the sector service entity; NPC grids saved; NPC blackboards are already rebuilt after a load (`PersistenceLoadSystem`).

**S4. Patrol, escort and inspection contracts.** CGP posts:
- patrols (visit beacons and POIs in order, scan ships on the way) that also lower local threat;
- escorts for NPC freighters (C4) and, as player-posted contracts, for player haulers and gas miners;
- inspections: board flagged ships to check licences, cargo manifests and contraband. Flags come from unlicensed sales (A7), black-market purchases and dead-drop hints.

Inspections give security a regulatory role between fights and use the framework's licences. They must be bounded: a ship can be inspected once per real day unless there is cause, and an inspection that finds nothing pays the inspected ship a small "inconvenience" credit, to keep it from becoming harassment.
- Effort: **medium**.
- Code: contract types; a "flagged" component on ships set by economy systems.
- Depends on: 5.2, framework licences.
- Persistence: flags as components with `DateTime`.

**S5. Generated investigations.** The detective gets PvE cases built from existing forensics: an NPC body or a burgled locker at a POI, with fingerprints, fibres and DNA on items, linked to an NPC suspect who has a station record (criminal records and DNA exist in `StationRecords`). Clues point to where the suspect hides (a POI or a ship). Closing the case (arrest and evidence turned in) pays the detective and the arresting officers. A few case templates (theft, murder, smuggling, fraud) with randomised details go a long way.
- Effort: **large** (generation of a believable scene and suspect is the work).
- Code: `Content.Server/_Kessler/Investigations/`; forensics (`Content.Server/Forensics/`), station records, NPC prototypes.
- Depends on: 5.2.
- Persistence: case record on the sector service entity; evidence and NPCs are saved entities. Check with a round-trip test that fingerprints, fibres and DNA on items survive a save.

**S6. Player-crime support, kept and fixed.** Keep outlaw jobs and pirates. Persist outlaw objective state (all `[ViewVariables]` today). Review payouts: 100,000 for killing a player and 400,000 for stealing a ship are large next to every other income, and on a persistent server they are paid for destroying another player's property that does not reset. Section 10 has the decision.
- Effort: **small** to persist; tuning is YAML.
- Code: `Content.Server/_WF/OutlawObjectives/OutlawObjectiveComponents.cs` (`[DataField]`, `DateTime` for `NextActivation`).
- Persistence: that is the fix.

**S7. Pay for performance.** Reduce the CGP account's ticking income (72,000/h) and pay security contracts from it instead, directly to the officer. A quiet day costs the account little; a busy day pays the people who worked.
- Effort: **small**.
- Code: `Resources/Prototypes/_NF/SectorServices/services.yml` and the contract board.
- Depends on: 5.2.

### 6.6 Service and station jobs

Goal: a new player who joins Dusk as station staff, janitor, mail carrier or chef on an empty server can earn a starter income alone, learn the game, and build `Station` reputation towards their first ship. In framework terms this is stage S0: 6–10k per hour, and `Station` L1 (120 reputation, about two hours of work) unlocks `PilotI` for Small ships.

**V1. The Dusk work-order board.** A console at Dusk and a PDA cartridge, fed by station needs (5.3) and NPC traffic, paid from the `WorkOrders` account per completed order to the worker at about 150 credits per expected minute. Orders are small (a few minutes each), plentiful, and capped per character per real day so ship owners do not farm them; low stages get the best rates. Each order gives about 1 `Station` reputation per expected minute. Job-specific order types:

| Job | Work orders | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| Janitor | Messes appear at Dusk over real time from NPC traffic (puddles, trash, grime); pest infestations; biohazard clean-ups at POIs after events | Spawn existing puddle and trash entities on a rate tied to station traffic; an order completes when its tiles are clean |
| Chef | NPC meal orders (ticket printed at the kitchen), catering contracts for POIs and ships, restocking food vendors | Order completes when the dish is placed on a delivery pad or sold through the kitchen hatch; quality bonus for complex recipes |
| Bartender | Drink orders at Club Calypso and Dusk bar, cocktail of the day, event nights | Same as chef |
| Botany (Theseus) | Produce orders for the kitchen and chemistry, seed requests | Procurement contracts; see V2 |
| Mail carrier | Parcels for players, NPC parcels for POIs, mail routes | C5 |
| Station staff | Any of the above, plus guest services (escorting new arrivals, cleaning cabins) | Umbrella role |
| Station representative | Adds bonuses to work orders from the Frontier account (replacing hand payroll through `StationWithdrawalPayroll`); approves large contracts; runs Dusk's budget | Gives the SR a management game |
| Librarian / archivist | Catalogue salvage data disks and logs into the library, write guides that the server rewards when approved, run the sector newsletter | Data disks from expeditions sold to the archive; book approvals by staff |

NPC "customers" do not need to be visible mobs. A printed ticket and a delivery pad are enough and cost nothing per tick. Visible NPC diners are a later polish.

- Effort: **medium** (board and order types on top of 5.2 and 5.3; each order type is small).
- Code: `Content.Server/_Kessler/StationWork/`; uses `Content.Server/Fluids/` for puddles, `Content.Server/Nutrition/` and kitchen systems for dishes, `Content.Server/_NF/Library/` for the archive.
- Depends on: 5.2, 5.3; framework `WorkOrders` account, S0 band and `Station` track.
- Persistence: orders in 5.2; messes and tickets are saved entities.

**V2. Botany genetics as long-term progression.** On a persistent server, plant genetics finally matter: seed traits (yield, potency, mutations) are stored on the seed and survive restarts, so a botanist can breed a strain over weeks. Add a seed bank at Theseus, rare seeds as framework rarity-tier loot from salvage, and contracts that ask for specific traits ("tomatoes with potency above 60"). Produce feeds the kitchen (V1), chemistry and medicine (med-sci doc).
- Effort: **medium**.
- Code: `Content.Server/Botany/` (upstream; check that `SeedData` on seed packets and in trays round-trips through a save), `Content.Server/_NF/Botany/`, contract types.
- Depends on: 5.2; framework rarity tiers; med-sci for medical plant inputs.
- Persistence: a round-trip test for seeds and plant holders is the first task.

**V3. Food and drink are worth something.** Give prepared food and drinks a value based on recipe complexity (a price on the recipe output, not only the reagents), with saturation (5.1) so a chef cannot flood cargo. Combined with V1 and station needs, cooking becomes a real income at small scale.
- Effort: **small** to **medium**.
- Code: `Content.Server/Cargo/Systems/PricingSystem.cs` hook or a Kessler `PriceCalculationEvent` handler; recipe tiers from `Resources/Prototypes/Recipes/Cooking/`.
- Depends on: 5.1.

**V4. Ship-side service.** Ship owners can hire a chef, bartender or janitor: a player-posted contract paying per real hour of service aboard. Small on top of 5.2, and it gives service players a path into ship life without owning a ship.

## 7. How departments feed each other

Who produces what for whom, once the proposals are in:

| Producer | Produces | Consumed by |
|---|---|---|
| Gas mining / atmos | Raw and refined gas, canisters to spec | Stations' life support (needs), medical (frezon, cryo gases), engineering (reactor fuel, TEG), cargo (hauling to unsaturated stations) |
| Salvage / mining | Ore, rare materials, wrecks, data disks | Theseus fabrication, engineering repairs, science (via archive), cargo |
| Botany | Produce, seeds, plant reagents | Kitchen, bar, chemistry, medical (rare chemistry inputs from bred strains) |
| Kitchen and bar | Meals, drinks | Station needs at Dusk, remote POIs, CGP outpost, ship crews |
| Engineering | Repairs, power, ship refits | Stations (maintenance), ship owners (after raids), cargo (power sales) |
| Cargo | Movement of everything, price information | Every department's contracts |
| Security | Lower threat, escorts, inspections, recovered goods | Haulers and gas miners in risky space; stations (fewer raids); the market (fewer smuggled goods) |
| Medical | Healing, medicine | Everyone after raids and hazards; station needs |
| Outlaws / pirates (players and NPC) | Risk, contraband, stolen cargo | Security work, black market, demand for escorts |

The loop that matters most for long-term play: **risky, valuable work (rich gas fields, far trade routes) attracts NPC pirates → needs security and escorts → produces damaged ships and injured crew → needs engineering and medical → which need materials and gas → which come from the risky places.** Station needs keep the bottom of the chain (service, cargo, maintenance) busy every day regardless of how many players are on.

### 8.3 Rarity tiers in these proposals

- **Common / Standard**: everything from work orders, mail, station needs, raw gas and ore, normal trade.
- **Superior**: Hazardous expeditions, rich gas-field loot, bred botany strains with top traits, refined-gas contract bonuses.
- **Rare**: Extreme expeditions, named pirate captains' loot, named derelicts (M3). Never from contracts' fixed rewards, mail, work orders or station needs; a contract may offer a limited-run blueprint instead (framework rule).
- **Exotic**: named derelicts only, under the framework's sector governor.
- Rare chemistry inputs, material data and field data that the medical-science design expects come from these same sources: hazardous gas fields, Extreme expeditions, named derelicts and bred botany strains.

### 8.5 Work orders and the `WorkOrders` account

All contracts in 5.2 and the Dusk board in V1 pay from `WorkOrders`. Payout = expected minutes × the holder's band rate (S0 about 150/min, S2 about 500/min). Department accounts receive a 10–20% cut instead of most of their flat tick, and heads spend them on equipment and bonuses.

### 8.6 Still open with the framework

1. **Starting balance against `PilotI`.** Players start with 30,000 (`HumanoidCharacterProfile.DefaultBalance`) and Small ships cost 20–50k, so money is not what holds a new player at Dusk; the `Station` L1 requirement (about two hours) is. If the owner wants "first days on station jobs", either lower the starting balance or raise `PilotI` to more than L1.
2. **Licence revocation**: an API to suspend a licence for N real days, used by CGP inspections.
3. **Track per contract, or per issuer**: this document assigns tracks per kind of work (8.1). If the framework prefers per issuing faction, a few rows move (gas to spec issued by Dusk would award `Station`).
4. **Ship-level reputation**: the medical-science design asks for reputation per ship or organisation. Escort and courier contracts would use it too (a trusted hauler company), so it should be one mechanism.

## 10. Open decisions for the owner

1. **Rebalance gas now or after saturation exists?**
   Recommendation: now. Cut raw gas prices (A1) and turn purity back on (A3) before launch. These are YAML changes, and cutting income after players have got used to it is much harder than starting low.

2. **Saturation per station or sector-wide?**
   Recommendation: per station, with a slow half-life (about a day). Per-station saturation creates hauling and trade routes; sector-wide saturation only lowers income.

3. **Where does contract money come from?**
   The framework settles the main part: a `WorkOrders` sector account whose tick scales with online players.
   Remaining decision: what happens to the existing flat department ticks (CGP 72k/h and the rest). Recommendation: cut them to a small base and give departments a 10–20% cut of contract payouts plus their existing taxes, so department money follows work done.

4. **Pay the worker or the department?**
   Recommendation: the worker, with a 10–20% cut to the department account for heads to spend on equipment and staff bonuses. Today's model (everything to the account, heads pay by hand) fails whenever no head is online, which at lowpop is most of the time.

5. **NPC raids on player ships: allowed?**
   Recommendation: yes, but only ships with crew aboard, only outside the safe zone around stations, and never stored, docked or SSD-held property. Raids should damage and steal cargo, not destroy ships outright. Without this, security has nothing to protect and risk does not scale with reward.

6. **Combat events at lowpop: lower the threshold or build the director first?**
   Recommendation: lower the thresholds now (to about 5 players) with smaller versions of the same events, and replace them with the director in phase 3.

7. **Outlaw objective payouts.**
   100,000 for killing a player and 400,000 for stealing a ship are large next to any other income, and on a persistent server they pay for destroying property that does not come back.
   Recommendation: cut them sharply, give outlaws PvE contracts (raid NPC freighters, smuggling) as their main income, and keep PvP objectives as rarer, smaller rewards that require the target to be in unsafe space.

8. **Do depleted gas deposits and asteroids come back?**
   Recommendation: individual deposits never regenerate; new gas fields appear on a rotation (A6) and old ones fade once empty. "This week's field" gives miners a reason to log in and scout.

9. **Can ship owners take Dusk work orders?**
   Recommendation: yes, but with lower pay and a tight daily cap above the first stage, so new players are never outcompeted for the work that is meant to get them started.

10. **Security as a staffed department at lowpop?**
    Recommendation: keep the CGP outpost and its jobs, but make most security contracts (patrols, pirate bounties, escorts) open to any licensed armed player. At five players online it is unlikely anyone is in a CGP job; the work still needs doing.

11. **Visible NPC customers and patrons?**
    Recommendation: not at first. Tickets and delivery pads are enough and cost no tick time. Revisit after measuring tick time with the persistent sector (`tickstats`).
